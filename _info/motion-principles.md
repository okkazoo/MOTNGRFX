# Motion principles

Distilled from 18 reviewed motion references (2026-10-22). Per-reference reviews live in `_inspiration/review/raivcoo_NN.md` (local only, gitignored). Bracketed numbers point to those reviews. Nothing here names a subject, brand or sequence: these are rules for making new work, not descriptions of existing work.

Use this two ways: (1) the planner applies the rules and numbers when it builds a plan; (2) the **idea engine** at the end turns a brief (a feel, inputs, music) into an original concept.

---

## 1. Constraint is the style

Every reference gets its identity from a few hard limits, not from many effects.

- **One primitive per piece or chapter.** Build everything from one repeated mark: a circle, a rounded rectangle, a dot, a tile, a card [03 05 09 11 12]. The rule becomes visible and form emerges from it.
- **One palette, small.** Two hues plus black and white [10 17], or 3–6 locked colours [16], or one hue varied only in texture and exposure [04]. The tighter the palette, the more extreme cutting and layout can get without falling apart [10].
- **One treatment over everything.** A single grade, print or photocopy texture unifies unrelated material, including generated and found images [04 06 18].
- **One shape language.** Same corner radius, line weight, geometry family throughout [12 16].

## 2. Anchors: something must stay put

When the background changes fast, the eye needs a fixed point.

- **Positional anchor:** keep one edge, horizon or focal point at the same screen position across every shot. Cuts can then be very fast [07]. Keep the centre of interest at frame centre while speed climbs [14].
- **Stable text layer:** captions and held words stay still, small and consistent while everything else moves [02 04 07]. Add words on cuts, not with animations [07].
- **Dominant constant shape:** one large flat shape stays; secondary elements pulse around it [18].
- **Shared ground:** a common background (often black) isolates unrelated styles and hides seams [13].

## 3. Seams: hide them or make them the show

Two strategies. Pick per piece.

**Hide the cut**
- In 2–5 frames of directional blur, overexposure or a whip; the next shot resolves out of the blur [04 06].
- With a **scale-matched hard cut**: same shape, size and centre on both sides, so the swap costs no time [14].
- By carrying the palette across the cut for a few frames [03], or changing only light between shots [01].

**Make the seam the event**
- **The outgoing scene's geometry becomes the incoming scene's frame** (a line, a hole, a disc) [13].
- **Shrink to a point or explode outward**, then grow the next scene from that point [09].
- **Hide the next scene inside the centre of the current one** and travel forward into it [14].
- **Morph by moving the same elements**, never by cross-fading; pass quickly through a compact, unreadable intermediate (blob, ring) [05 11].
- **One conversion device as the transition** (dots, grid, scanlines, tiles): every image passes through it, which makes dissimilar material look designed [02 15].
- **Directional grid wipe** with a visible edge travelling across the frame, about 0.5 s [15].

## 4. Rhythm and music

- **Cut on hits, not the grid.** Onsets and section changes are what the ear feels; a hit-aligned cut feels tighter than a beat-perfect one [06 13]. Measured: cuts landed on audio onsets 64% vs 29% chance [06].
- **Scene changes on bar lines; bursts at phrase ends.** Hard cuts on every 4th beat, then 3–6 very short cuts at the end of a phrase, held together by one repeated centred element [16].
- **Cut rate follows the energy curve.** Shot length shrinks as the music builds (about 1 s down to 0.2 s) [07 13]. Cut rate itself reads as intensity.
- **Voice leads? Cut to the phrase,** not the beat [02].
- **Light is the fastest rhythm cue.** Exposure strobe on the beat: dip 4 frames, spike 2–4 [17].
- **Asymmetric punches.** Scale up on the hit, ease back over the rest of the beat, like a drum strike [17]. Pulses attack in 2–3 frames and decay over 4–8 [18].
- **Vary shot length (0.2–2 s).** Irregular timing reads as emphasis; a fixed grid reads as a metronome [10].
- **Long pieces: let the music be a bed.** Scene length (3–6 s) carries the pace; loose sync avoids fatigue [09]. A reel can repeat a fixed 4–8 s segment length and make the handover the repeated element [13].
- **Silent pieces need a visual pulse**: constant step length [11].
- **Verify tempo from the picture**: trackers make 2× and 2:3 errors that drift a whole edit [17].

## 5. Energy architecture

How a piece rises and lands. Shared shapes across the references:

- **Start small in the dark**: one element appears and grows; attention locks onto the only visible thing [01].
- **Hold, then drop**: slow shots first, fast cuts after one musical impact, so the drop feels big [04].
- **Bursts need rests**: 1 s near-black rests after bursts [02], 6–9 s calm between burst groups [18], a near-silent black dip before the densest passage [13], 0.3 s bare-ground resets between items [15].
- **A longer hold inside a fast run** makes the cuts around it feel faster [06].
- **Accelerate monotonically** in short pieces: fewer frames per event in each segment, arrival without a drop [14].
- **Chapters by colour**: change palette and ground brightness per chapter; alternate dark and light chapters [09 16]. A colour curve (saturation and brightness rising, then dropping) can carry the arc with no content change [01].
- **Endings go still**: stop on the biggest sound change [07], a calm single mark after the loudest moment [10], picture and sound stopping together [09], a long silent hold [13]. Final titles far smaller than feels safe [01].
- **Loops**: last frame equals the first [07 08 11 12]; or return to the opening palette [03].

## 6. Motion and easing

- **One slow movement per shot** in calm pieces; uniform drift lets cuts pass unnoticed [01].
- **Never freeze a hold**: keep slow residual drift [05 12 15].
- **Hold longer than you move.** Readable states hold 2–4× as long as the transition (about ⅓ motion, ⅔ hold per beat) [05 11]. Bursts around 0.7 s, holds around 1.4 s [08].
- **Easing matches mood.** Calm: long ease-in-out, no overshoot [12]. Playful or physical: squash and stretch on entry, settle to normal [08]. Percussive: fast attack, slow release [17].
- **Stagger groups by 2–4 frames** so a group move becomes a wave [12].
- **Trails only when fast**: motion trails communicate speed and disappear when not needed [05].
- **Echo for richness**: repeat an object with fixed time delays and simple motion becomes complex form [03].
- **Parallax gives flat material volume**: move the camera through layers at different speeds [02].
- **Give motion a cause**: a press, squash or impact plus a 3–4 frame spiky pop starts a journey [14].

## 7. Type

- **Text holds still; the world moves** [04 07]. Change its colour per background for legibility, never its size or position within a word [07].
- **Editing as animation**: remove letters to reveal a second message; colour the survivors before the rest go [08].
- **Containers refit every frame**: a plate or outline that shrinks with its text feels physical [08].
- **Larger than the frame**: cropped giant type signals energy without motion [10]. Repeated rows sliding at different speeds, easing into alignment for the reading moment [10].
- **Disorder must resolve**: every scramble or dropout ends in a legible phrase held for a few frames [10].
- **Scale contrast is hierarchy**: one typeface, huge for titles, tiny for credits [16].

## 8. Colour and texture

- **Restrict, then accent**: one hot accent on a reduced image survives strobing and directs the eye [08 17].
- **Grain or dither on gradients** removes banding and gives a print feel [03]. Analogue artefacts (grain, dust, halation, vignette) read as human [04].
- **Gradient fill with a bright rim on a dark ground** fakes depth cheaply [03]. A thin coloured edge-glow carries identity while fills stay quiet [12].
- **Switch the ground colour** between scenes: separation without a transition effect [16].
- **Silhouettes against light** read in half a second [06].
- **Mix soft analogue blur with a few hard digital blocks**: contrast of textures, not more elements [01].

## 9. Content devices

- **Make the effect out of the subject** (the tiles are the thing): the idea explains itself in one viewing [15].
- **Show the metaphor**: transform one object into its counterpart by shape [16].
- **Put the abstract into the real**: place a flat graphic inside real photography near the end [09].
- **Build form from tiny marks**: dot density and contour spacing alone convey 3D shape [11].
- **Repeat a grammar**: one 3-beat pattern (real, transform, hold) per item teaches the viewer fast, so variations feel clever [15].

## The idea engine

How to get from a brief to an original concept without copying any reference.

**Inputs:** the feel (reference images, words), the user's material (text, images, audio), the duration and format.

1. **Read the feel as constraints, not content.** Extract: palette (3–6 colours), texture (paper, grain, print, glass…), composition habit (centred, layered, diagonal, collaged…), scale contrast, mood words. Ignore the subjects.
2. **Fix one constraint per axis** (section 1): one primitive, one palette, one treatment, one shape language, and one anchor (section 2).
3. **Choose one conceit.** The single idea a viewer could describe in a sentence. Build it by pairing a **content device** (section 9) with a **seam device** (section 3) that did not appear together in any one reference. Transform at least one principle into a different domain (e.g. a seam device used on type, a type device used on images).
4. **Map the music to an arc** (section 5). Pick one: *dark-grow → peak → still end*; *hold → drop → bursts with rests*; *steady chapters by colour*; *monotonic acceleration*; *seamless loop*. If a voice is present, cut to its phrases.
5. **Apply rhythm and motion rules** (sections 4, 6) with the numbers table.
6. **Originality check** before committing:
   - No subject, sequence or signature move of a single reference reproduced.
   - Principles drawn from at least three different references.
   - At least one principle applied in a new domain.
   - Can state in one sentence how it differs from every reference.

**In the app:** sections 1–9 become planner rules and style-family parameters; the idea engine becomes the LLM planner's reasoning steps (design_llm / Claude), with the originality check as a self-review before the plan JSON is emitted.
