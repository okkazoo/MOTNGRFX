=== TRANSITIONS: HOW THE LEAD DESIGNER CHOOSES THEM (learn the principle, never copy a value; every piece is made new) ===
10. EVERY BOUNDARY IS A DECISION. A piece with several shots has a boundary between each pair; `compose.cuts` is where you say what happens at it, and a boundary with no entry is the plain default (a soft crossfade).
   Between words there is a second kind of transition (`type.transitions`, one entry per gap): strands that carry one word into the next. The two kinds are separate choices, and the best moments link them: the word is the thing that carries the cut.
   - ON THE BEAT. A cut lands on a downbeat, a chord change or a dropout of the music analysis, never between beats. The word before it holds to the cut or just past it; the next word forms after it.
   - PLACE CHANGE versus CONTINUATION. A boundary where the next shot starts from a new still (a new place) is where the heavy tools belong: a 3D transition (`depth_pivot`, `orbit:<mode>`; `depth_dolly` is off: it renders as a shard collage), a text window (`window_cut`,
     `window_swap`, `window_other`) or a `pan:<dir>`. A boundary where the next shot continues the last frame of the previous one (`first: last:<id>`) is the SAME place: a hard cut (or nothing). A 3D move across a continuation is wasted and the code turns it into a hard cut.
   - DIFFERENT AT EVERY CUT. A piece with three or more place changes uses a different transition at each: not the same kind twice, not the same kind with the same direction twice. Mix families (one 3D, one window, one hard cut) rather than
     one tool everywhere. The code replaces a repeat with the next unused fitting kind and says so; write the variety yourself and it keeps your choice and your reason.
   - MATCH THE MOTION. A directional kind (`pan`) goes the way the outgoing shot already moves: a runner leaving right, a camera drifting right, a rise = up. Write the direction of the picture's own motion; if you give no direction the code
     reads it from the shot prompt. `orbit:push` is for a build (the music rises into the cut), `orbit:pullback` for a release (the music drops after it), `orbit:fly` for a steady, free cut.
   - THE SUBJECT STAYS IN FRONT. In every 3D transition the subject stays crisp in front of the type while the background transforms; the type sits between them and the word before the cut leaves, and the word after it arrives, in the direction of the move
     (the code sets those sides). So a shot that has a 3D transition needs a chain that puts the type BEHIND the subject (`sandwich` with the matte): write the chain of the two shots that way.
   - TEMPO. Slow music wants slower, simpler transitions: a pullback or a pivot rather than a fly, one 3D cut instead of three; a fast piece can renew the picture with a different move every two bars. Each 3D transition takes 3-4 s of footage around the cut
     (the shot before needs 1.2 s, the shot after 1.8 s), so a piece of short shots uses fewer of them.
   - LINKED, WHEN YOU CAN. Prefer the transitions that the word carries: `window_other` (a heavy word held just before a place change shows the NEXT shot inside its letters), `window_cut` (the word opens and grows until the next shot fills the frame),
     `window_swap` (on the cut beat the two pictures swap places), or a 3D move with the words sliding the same way. They need a heavy liked face, a word at least 30 % of the frame tall and two shots that differ in light; at most two window transitions per piece.
   - READABLE HOLDS BETWEEN. A transition never eats a word: keep each word readable for at least 1.2 s before and after the transition window, and do not put two transitions on neighbouring boundaries.
   - TEXT TRANSITIONS. In `type.transitions` use a different strand transition in every gap (cut excepted), long ones (braid, spiral_around, circuit_board) in slow pieces and short ones (twist, fan, ripple, cut) in fast ones; `cut` where a word
     ends on a dropout. A word that ends a shot hands over through the cut instead of a strand transition when the boundary carries a window or 3D transition.
