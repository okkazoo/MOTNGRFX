=== PRINCIPLES OF THE LEAD DESIGNER'S PIECES (learn the principle, never copy a value; every piece is made new) ===
1. ONE FILMABLE IDEA, written together with the words. The footage is not a backdrop invented separately: the idea names what the camera does FOR the type. Each shot prompt says what the subject does in THAT shot,
   progresses the previous shot (place, light, action) and states ONE camera move chosen for the kind of subject. A subject that crosses the frame (a cyclist, a horse) is tracked so that it stays at the same place in the frame
   (the matte and the type stay stable); a subject that approaches (a stranger, a dog) is met by a camera that backs away and keeps it in frame; a landscape gets one slow lateral sweep. Shot prompts are concrete, photographic and different from each other.
2. ONE SYSTEM. Pick one rule and develop it instead of changing everything on every word. Typical systems: one module for the whole piece with the variety coming from the fonts and axes; two fonts that alternate strictly
   (A, B, A, B) with the yaw alternating -n / +n; one font axis that is animated on the beat and PROGRESSES across the words (each word's lo-hi window higher than the last); a palette that changes per section on the
   chord changes. Fonts are liked fonts only (at least three distinct, one of them expressive); a system uses them as ROLES that recur in a pattern. Three or four modules in five words is noise, not a system.
3. THE TYPE MOVES, IN PROPORTION TO THE MUSIC. A static word is a failure, but the amount of motion belongs to the piece: it follows the tempo and the mood. `beat_orbit` is the camera of the type world;
   `intro_deg` is how far off face-on a word starts and swings in from, `fast_dps` the turn speed in the gaps, `bob` the breathing, `read_dps` the drift while it is read. A kinetic, fast piece (110 bpm and up) swings hard
   (intro_deg 35-55, fast_dps 40-60, yaw 14-22, bob about 1); a slow, filmic or tender piece (under 90 bpm) moves slowly and little (intro_deg 12-25, fast_dps 8-20, yaw 5-10, bob 0.4-0.6, read_dps 3-4); a middle tempo
   sits between. Yaw alternates in sign from word to word; at least one font axis is animated on the beat. `hold_drift` or yaw 0 on every word reads as a slideshow; the same camera numbers for every piece is a template.
4. TIMING IS MUSICAL, NOT MECHANICAL. Hits are chosen on musical moments (a chord change, the bar a section starts, the first downbeat after a dropout), not on every Nth downbeat. 1.2 s is the FLOOR of a hold, never the
   target: the word that carries the meaning holds 1.8-3 s, a light word less, the last word the longest; the hold of a word is what is left between its hit and the next word's form-in, not a constant. Form and exit times differ
   too (a slow form into a held word, a fast exit on a dropout). On a dropout of the music, freeze the picture (`compose.holds`, 0.3-0.8 s) and flip the plate. Never the same gap and the same hold on every word.
5. THE COMPOSE CHAIN IS WRITTEN FOR THE SCENE. A chain of 8-30 ops, not the default three. The ingredients the lead designer combines: a graded footage (`grade`: darker, desaturated for black and white); a type colour
   sampled from the footage (`tint_type`); the type sandwiched between footage and subject (`sandwich`) with `matte_gain` switching per word between behind and in front; a darkened halo behind the letters for legibility
   (`blur` of the type matte -> `crossfade` footage to a dark `fill`); a glow whose COLOUR changes per section (a step timeline) and whose strength follows the music (`curve`); a flat plate with letters as windows (`knockout`)
   whose colour flips black/white on the dropouts; contour lines or scribbles around the subject drawn on the beat; flow of the footage pushing the type (`flow_wake`) only where it fits; grain and vignette last.
   Different shots get different chains when the scenes differ: each shot (or each pair of shots of one location) has its own `compose.shots.<id>.ops` with its own glow / plate colour, its own grade and its own treatment
   (one chain copied to every shot is the default, not a design). The chain reads only what the shot has (`$matte` when the subject can be cut out, `$depth`, `$flow`).
6. PALETTE IS TIED TO THE SHOTS AND THE MUSIC: one accent per section/location, used by the glow, the plate and the type tint, switching on the chord change; a black-and-white picture stays black and white with at most one accent.
7. EVERY LOCKUP CARRIES MEANING. Split the phrase into beats that each say something (HOLD / ON TO / NOTHING), never a weak function word alone (THE, OF, AND on a line of its own is a failure unless it opens
   a longer line). Break long words into 2-3 stacked lines of at least 3 letters.
8. THE REPLY IS THE WHOLE PIECE. `idea` names the single idea; `system` states the rule, the roles and the progression; the numbers in `type` and `compose` then follow from them. Do not copy example values from this guide.
9. SHOT STRUCTURE FOLLOWS THE STORY AND THE TEMPO. What the engine supports: a shot starts from a still (`first: "A"`) or continues the last frame of an earlier shot (`first: "last:<id>"`, the camera move carried on, the prompt
   progresses the action); shots are joined by a hard cut on a downbeat (`compose.crossfade` 0); the app plans at most 6 shots; a shot whose chain reads `$matte` is at most 145 frames (6 s: the matte generator's limit), a plain
   shot at most 385 frames (16 s). ONE scene with ONE camera move for the whole piece is ONE long shot, and only then. A piece that moves through places or beats is several shots: each place gets its own still and its own camera move,
   a place that lasts longer than one shot is continued by a second shot that starts from the first one's last frame, and the cut to the next place lands on a downbeat (a chord change or the start of a section). Shot length follows
   the tempo: a fast piece (130 bpm and up) renews the picture about every two bars (~3 s at 160 bpm): a place that lasts six seconds is two shots of three, the second continuing the first; a middle tempo uses about two bars (~5 s);
   a slow piece (under 80 bpm) lets a place breathe for four bars or more, or is one shot. Do not collapse a story with beats into one long shot, and do not cut where one continuous move would do.
